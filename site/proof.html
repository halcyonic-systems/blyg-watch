Why Lean, and why now

Blygger is pre-1.0, and its spec says to assume "no promises" until then. That is the cheapest time to check a design: its guarantees are short statements, its eleven clients are not, and a contradiction found now can still change the spec. One has already turned up: §16.8 refuses a mention relation for links, while §10.3a and §15.4 add one.

PrecedentWhat it shows
Buterin, 2026
Formal verification pays off where "the goal is much simpler than the implementation", and works by stating intent several ways and checking they agree. blyg-watch states it three ways: the model, the Lean, the crawl.
Newcombe et al., Amazon, 2015
Checking designs with TLA+ caught serious bugs before code shipped, one needing a trace of 35 high-level steps.
Cremers et al., TLS 1.3, 2016
A machine-checked analysis of a draft found an attack the working group confirmed before the standard was final.
Cedar, Amazon, 2024
A Lean model with proofs, paired with testing the real implementation against it. blyg-watch pairs Lean with a crawl of real blygs.
Gomes, Kleppmann et al., 2017
Replicated-data algorithms proven in Isabelle, after many published ones turned out wrong. The closest precedent for a decentralized system's guarantees.

On names: "Lean Ethereum" means a leaner protocol, a simplification program with formal verification as one of its values (Drake, 2025). Lean the prover is the tool some of that work uses: the strawmap (updated 23 October 2026) plans cryptography specs and the consensus state transition function in Lean 4. Venkatesh Rao kept Blygger's spec minimal on purpose; stating its guarantees formally is the same simplify-then-verify pairing, at much smaller stakes.

What this step will hold

Recoverable links
Which links a crawl made later can still find, from latest and pinned versions.
Links only a snapshot keeps
Which links are lost to a later crawl, for example through an unpinned older version or a withdrawal. §8.4, §9
Where it will live
The proof journal, checked by Lean every time the site is built.

What is not claimed

  • The Lean work models spec v0.4 as written, a pre-1.0 draft.
  • It says nothing about any client, or about whether a publisher keeps its promises. Hosting can lapse.
  • Nothing is proven until Lean checks it when the journal is built.
